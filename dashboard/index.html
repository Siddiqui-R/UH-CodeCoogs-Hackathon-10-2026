Houston storm evidence, 2017–2024

When the power went out, the information went out with it.

Hurricane Beryl cut power to millions of Houston-area customers in July 2024. Four of the five official reports reviewed here name unreliable information as a core failure. Outages: one snapshot per day (time of day not stated) for each ZIP code, from an organizer-supplied file with no named publisher. Each ZIP counts toward the county holding most of its area, using simplified county outlines; ZIPs that straddle a county line are flagged in the downloads. FEMA counts are modeled housing-damage assessments, not insured claims or dollars. The two outage sources disagree and neither covers every utility: EAGLE-I is far lower for Harris but higher for Montgomery. The source's customer totals also change from day to day, so percentages are approximate.

A threshold counts only once outages stay below it for the rest of the data, so one-off source dropouts (e.g. Harris EAGLE-I, Jul 9 8 p.m.) are ignored. “Not reached” means the data ends first; nothing is extrapolated. Harvey uses daily ZIP snapshots (days); Beryl uses EAGLE-I hourly means (hours). PUCT reports CenterPoint's full Beryl restoration took 11 days.
